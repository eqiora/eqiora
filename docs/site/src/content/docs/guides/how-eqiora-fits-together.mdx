title: How Eqiora fits together
description: Place equations, numerical choices, execution, and observations at the right step of a modeling workflow.
---

Eqiora keeps the mathematical problem separate from the choices needed to solve
it. The [Get started example](/get-started/) shows every step with one decay model.

## Model — what must be true?

Declare quantities, parameters, initial values and equations. Compilation checks
their meaning and produces a Model. A time method, mesh or solver is not an equation.
Read [Models are not simulations](/learn/mathematical-modeling/models-not-simulations/)
for the distinction, or [author a model in Python](/guides/modeling/).

## Realization — how will it be solved?

Choose numerical methods that support your model. In the decay example,
`eqiora.resolve` selects a time method and returns a Plan. Spatial problems also
need the appropriate geometry, mesh and discretization; the
[steady-flow investigation](/gallery/exact-cylinder-steady-stokes/) shows those
choices on one concrete problem.

## Run — perform the work

Supply the initial state, observation times and other execution inputs required
by the Plan, then call `eqiora.run`. The
[execution guide](/guides/execution-and-arrays/) explains submitted runs and structured diagnostics.

## Result — what happened?

Select an output using the model-bound field handle. For scalar decay, the
returned series contains observation times and values. Compare them with a
prediction before interpreting a plot. [Run and inspect](/guides/run-and-inspect/)
walks through the same decay example.

To decide whether a model answers your physical question, examine its
assumptions and compare its predictions with a case you understand. The
[Learn textbooks](/learn/) build those habits while deriving each model.

## Looking inside the implementation?

The [contributor architecture overview](/contributing/architecture/) describes
