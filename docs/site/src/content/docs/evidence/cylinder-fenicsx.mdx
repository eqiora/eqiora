Eqiora and FEniCSx passed the independently frozen agreement gates on all three
shared cylinder meshes. **Eqiora was slower in ordinary execution at every level.**
On the finest mesh, the observed medians were **8.721 s for Eqiora and 0.6201 s
for FEniCSx**. These are one shared-host campaign's measurements, not a stable
baseline, a general ranking, or a continuum drag-accuracy result.

The optimized Eqiora development wheel used here is distinct from the published
release. The actual wheel producer, campaign harness and external-reference
harness have separate identities in the linked record. No GPU was used.

## Problem and accuracy

The finite channel is 2.2 m long and 0.41 m high, with a radius-0.05 m hole
centered at (0.2 m, 0.2 m). Dynamic viscosity is 0.001 Pa·s. The inlet is
parabolic with maximum speed 0.3 m/s; walls and cylinder are no-slip. The outlet
has zero full-stress traction. This is linear steady Stokes flow with MINI
P1-plus-bubble velocity and P1 pressure, with no added pressure gauge.

Every tool uses the same original affine mesh, vertex/cell identities and
boundary partition. The reference is FEniCSx on that discrete problem:
A=MUMPS/Duffy quadrature, B=SuperLU/the same quadrature, and C=MUMPS/independent
degree-six quadrature. Each mesh's empirical budgets and material falsifiers
were frozen before Eqiora ran. No Navier–Stokes/DFG number is used as a Stokes
expectation.

The complete gate checks vertex velocity, cell bubbles, vertex pressure,
independently assembled equilibrium, cylinder force pair, essential trace and
discrete inlet/continuity fluxes. Three separate zero-solve reconstructions also
passed cylinder-force, global essential-reaction and flux checks on imported
Eqiora cold fields. The cylinder force uses volume virtual work, not raw surface
stress integration. No zero-lift assumption is made: the cylinder is offset
from the channel midline.

Six fresh workers each made one cold call, one separate profile call, two
warmups and seven ordinary samples: 60 ordinary calls and six profile calls,
with 42 retained samples. Every ordinary call assembled a new system and used
new direct factors. Eqiora used its ordinary `run(plan)` and faer sparse LU;
FEniCSx used designated policy A with PETSc/MUMPS. Solver implementations and
internal phase boundaries differ.

The ordinary boundaries include assembly, accepted solve and field return.
FEniCSx also explicitly recovers its mixed field, computes physical/scaled true
residual summaries and destroys its fresh system before returning. Mesh/model
preparation, JIT work, public force/flux inspection, field export and independent
accuracy checks are outside both clocks. No solved matrix, factor or solution
is reused.

## Mesh setup and separate phases

These are single measurements from each fresh worker and its separate profile
call. They are not ordinary-sample medians and must not be added to the ordinary
median. Mesh setup reopens/checks the fixed mesh in Eqiora and constructs the
DOLFINx mesh, MINI space, boundaries and basis maps in FEniCSx; it does not time
Gmsh generation.

Preparation means model compilation/resolution in Eqiora and form/JIT compilation
in FEniCSx. Eqiora assembly is native MINI system finalization; linear includes
symbolic/numeric factorization and its two backsolves. FEniCSx assembly includes
basis congruence and lifting; linear includes new KSP/MUMPS setup, solve, residual
summaries and solver/vector cleanup. Its field recovery and matrix cleanup are
recorded separately. No cross-tool internal-phase equivalence is claimed.

## Environment and reproduction

This run used one thread/rank on an Intel Xeon Gold 6346 shared host, with worker
CPU 20 and supervisor CPU 21; their SMT siblings are 52 and 53. GPUs were hidden.
Initial load average was about 30, siblings were not reserved, and frequency
was not fixed. CPU/governor/frequency/process snapshots and sampled descendants
and load are retained. These conditions do not establish a stable reference
machine.

FEniCSx used DOLFINx/Basix/FFCx 0.11.0 and PETSc 3.25.6 from the locked environment.
