## From an equation to a reusable part

Each investigation starts with a question and a prediction you can make by
hand. Derive the equations, express the model in `.eqi`, and read the result.
Then open the reusable components: their equations explain how the same model
can be written more concisely. Change a material, a boundary, or a parameter,
and predict the consequence before running again.

Keep physical assumptions and numerical choices separate. A change in thermal
conductivity changes the model; a finer mesh changes how you approximate it.
The exercises help you explore both.

## Follow an idea into another subject

- **Conservation:** follow energy through [heat transfer](/learn/heat-transfer/),
  power through [circuits](/learn/circuits-dynamics/), and momentum through
  [fluid mechanics](/learn/fluid-mechanics/).
- **Stress and work:** connect [loads in a solid](/learn/structural-mechanics/)
  with [forces in a fluid](/learn/fluid-mechanics/).
- **Prediction and measurement:** solve a [thermal problem](/learn/heat-transfer/),
  then ask what its temperature reveals in [inverse problems](/learn/inverse-problems/).
- **Approximation:** use [numerical simulation](/learn/numerical-simulation/)
  to understand time steps, spatial resolution, and the difference between a
  small residual and an accurate solution.
