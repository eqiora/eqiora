## Why relations come before algorithms

Many models are networks of simultaneous statements rather than recipes that
calculate one variable after another. Conservation at a junction, compatibility
between connected parts, and a constitutive law can all constrain the same
unknowns. Calling one variable an “input” too early can hide that structure.

Eqiora writes each statement as a residual equal to zero. An equation such as
$a+b=s$ therefore has the residual $a+b-s$. The ordering of the symbols does
not assign a computational direction.

## Learning outcomes

After this chapter, you should be able to:

- distinguish unknown quantities, prescribed parameters, and relations;
- count unknowns and independent scalar relations in a small model;
- separate conservation from the law that closes a network;
- compile an algebraic `.eqi` model without selecting a solver; and
- identify underdetermined, overconstrained, and inconsistent structures.

## What fixes the temperature between two layers?

Imagine two thermal resistances in series between reservoirs at $T_h$ and
$T_c$. The unknown interface temperature is $T_m$. Each resistance is a
lumped relation $\dot Q=\Delta T/R$, where $R$ has units kelvins per watt.
Assume no energy accumulates at the interface and no heat escapes sideways.

```text
T_h ── [ R₁ ] ── T_m ── [ R₂ ] ── T_c
       Q̇₁ →              Q̇₂ →
```

*Conceptual network. Both arrows point from the hot reservoir toward the cold
reservoir; steady interface balance requires equal signed powers.*

There are three unknowns: $T_m$, $\dot Q_1$, and $\dot Q_2$. Two constitutive
relations and one balance close the problem:

$$
\dot Q_1=\frac{T_h-T_m}{R_1},\qquad
\dot Q_2=\frac{T_m-T_c}{R_2},\qquad
\dot Q_1-\dot Q_2=0.
$$

Eliminating the powers gives

$$
T_m=\frac{R_2T_h+R_1T_c}{R_1+R_2},\qquad
\dot Q=\frac{T_h-T_c}{R_1+R_2}.
$$

For $T_h=340\,\mathrm K$, $T_c=300\,\mathrm K$,
$R_1=1\,\mathrm{K/W}$, and $R_2=3\,\mathrm{K/W}$, predict
$\dot Q=10\,\mathrm W$ and $T_m=330\,\mathrm K$. The larger resistance takes
the larger temperature drop. The [heat-transfer path](/learn/heat-transfer/)
derives a slab's resistance from a spatial field rather than assuming it.

The abstract splitter below keeps the same distinction between balance and
closure while making the source small enough to inspect at once.

## A two-branch split

Let a prescribed amount $s$ split into two unknown branch amounts $a$ and $b$.
Conservation provides one relation,

$$
a+b-s=0.
$$

That relation alone cannot determine both branches. For a symmetric splitter,
the constitutive or design assumption $a-b=0$ supplies the second relation.
Solving the two statements by substitution gives $a=b=s/2$, but the model owns
the relations—not the elimination order used to obtain that expression.

## Count before solving

For this bounded example:

| Item | Count | Role |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Prescribed parameters | 1 | `supplied` is known for one model instance. |
| Scalar unknowns | 2 | `branch_a` and `branch_b` must be determined. |
| Scalar relations | 2 | conservation and equal split close this particular structure. |

Matching counts are a useful first check, not a guarantee. Two copies of the conservation
relation would still leave the split undetermined. A third relation demanding
`branch_a = supplied` would contradict equal split when `supplied` is nonzero.
Independence and consistency depend on relation content, not names or counts.

## From equations to networks

In a physical network, a component typically owns its constitutive relation,
while a connection owns compatibility or conservation across ports. This makes
the model acausal: the assembled relations determine which quantities must be
solved together. Connection order does not turn into execution order: the assembled equations
must be solved consistently.

## Once the equations are understood, import the parts

The electrical counterpart makes reusable components concrete. For two
resistors in parallel across ideal voltage $V$, equal voltage across both
branches is compatibility, $I_j=V/R_j$ is the component law, and
$I_s=I_1+I_2$ is junction conservation. These roles follow the circuit reasoning
in [OpenStax, section 10.3](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/10-3-kirchhoffs-rules).

For an illustrative $V=6\,\mathrm V$, $R_1=2\,\Omega$, and $R_2=4\,\Omega$,
the hand prediction is $I_1=3\,\mathrm A$, $I_2=1.5\,\mathrm A$, and total
current $4.5\,\mathrm A$. The supplied power is $27\,\mathrm W$, equal to
$18+9\,\mathrm W$ dissipated in the two resistors. This second check asks about
energy as well as charge.

## Read the result

Interpret the two residuals against the hand solution $a=b=s/2$. Each must
vanish separately: their sum could hide a positive error in one relation and a
negative error in the other. The compiler check prepares the source; substitution
is what establishes the solution here.

## Deliberate failure: remove the closing relation

Delete `equal_split` while retaining conservation. The source remains readable
and dimensionally consistent, so the current compile check need not diagnose
the missing physical assumption. By hand, one equation constrains two unknowns:
every pair satisfying $a+b=s$ is possible. The repair is to state a justified
second relation, not to choose an arbitrary solver default.

This is an important diagnostic lesson: successful compilation does not prove
that a model is closed or physically meaningful.

## Exercises

1. For $s=1$, list three distinct pairs $(a,b)$ that satisfy conservation when
   `equal_split` is absent.
2. Replace equal split with $a=2b$. Derive both branch amounts without changing
   the conservation relation.
3. Add the relation $a=s$ to the original symmetric model. For which value of
   $s$ are all three relations consistent?
4. Give one circuit or flow-network example in which a connection owns
   conservation and a component owns a constitutive law. Identify every
   unknown and prescribed parameter.

### Exercise guidance

Exercise 1 has the family $(a,b)=(\alpha,1-\alpha)$; nonnegative branch amounts
would additionally require $0\le\alpha\le1$. Exercise 2 gives $a=2s/3$ and
$b=s/3$. In exercise 3, consistency requires $s=0$.

For a further modeling experiment, give the thermal interface a finite heat
capacity $C_m$. Conservation becomes $C_m\dot T_m=\dot Q_1-\dot Q_2$. The
algebraic relations for the resistances stay unchanged, but you now need an
initial temperature. Explain why steady state recovers the result above.
