How long does a warm object take to cool? What temperature lies between two
layers of insulation? Why does changing a boundary condition change the answer?
These questions lead from physical assumptions to equations you can explain,
solve by hand in simple cases, and express in Eqiora.

This path assumes algebra and introductory calculus. You can read its
mathematics without running code. If you already have a physical subject in
mind, start with [heat transfer](/learn/heat-transfer/),
[fluid mechanics](/learn/fluid-mechanics/),
[structures](/learn/structural-mechanics/), or
[circuits and dynamics](/learn/circuits-dynamics/), and use these chapters when
you need a modeling idea. They are a companion, not an entrance requirement.

## A connected investigation

Begin with a bead whose whole temperature changes together. Derive its energy
balance, normalize it into scalar decay, and compare a run with the exponential
solution. Then ask what happens inside a thick wall, where one temperature is
no longer enough. Fields, conservation, material laws, and boundary conditions
turn that question into a spatial model.

Along the way, write the small equations directly before opening the maintained
components that package the same relationships. An import becomes meaningful
when you can identify the assumptions and equations it saves you from repeating.
Each chapter ends with a deliberate mistake, exercises, and guidance to help
you reason beyond a successful run.

## Build a model

1. [Models are not simulations](/learn/mathematical-modeling/models-not-simulations/) — derive a cooling balance and distinguish changing the physical assumptions from changing how a computer solves it.
2. [Quantities, dimensions, and units](/learn/mathematical-modeling/quantities-dimensions-units/) — check power and time scales, distinguish temperature from a temperature difference, and normalize a model without losing its meaning.
3. [Algebraic relations and networks](/learn/mathematical-modeling/algebraic-relations-networks/) — derive a two-layer thermal network, count independent relations, and recognize conservation and material laws in imported electrical parts.
4. [Ordinary differential equations](/learn/mathematical-modeling/ordinary-differential-equations/) — derive and run exponential decay, interpret its characteristic time, and ask what changes when the environment moves.

## Extend it through space

5. [Fields and spatial domains](/learn/mathematical-modeling/fields-spatial-domains/) — replace one temperature with a field, calculate a gradient, and read the supports and boundary names of a flow model.
6. [Conservation laws](/learn/mathematical-modeling/conservation-laws/) — account for storage, outward flux, and generation; derive a local heat balance and recover the lumped model by integration.
7. [Constitutive laws](/learn/mathematical-modeling/constitutive-laws/) — derive slab resistance from Fourier conduction, test stress laws in simple motions, and inspect reusable material components.
8. [Boundary and interface conditions](/learn/mathematical-modeling/boundary-interface-conditions/) — connect the environment to the equations, join two materials, and recognize missing references or contradictory data.
