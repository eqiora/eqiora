## The question

A tank is motionless, yet the pressure on its wall grows with depth. Before
simulating moving water, predict that pressure and the force on a vertical gate.
This establishes the sign and units of the stresses we will later compute.

## A small region containing many molecules

A continuum model assigns density $\rho$, velocity $\boldsymbol u$ and pressure
$p$ to points. A point represents an average over a region containing many
molecules, small compared with the length on which the flow varies. The model
loses this interpretation if molecular scales become comparable to the geometry.

For the initial route, assume a single Newtonian fluid with constant density and
viscosity, no free-surface motion and no temperature dependence. These assumptions
are suitable for studying the equations; they must be reconsidered for a boiling
liquid, a rarefied gas or a strongly compressible flow.

| Quantity | Meaning | SI unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\rho$ | Mass per volume | $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ |
| $p$ | Isotropic compressive stress | $\mathrm{Pa=kg/(m\,s^2)}$ |
| $\mu$ | Dynamic viscosity | $\mathrm{Pa\,s}$ |
| $\nu=\mu/\rho$ | Momentum diffusivity | $\mathrm{m^2/s}$ |

Pressure and viscosity are different physical quantities. Pressure can exist at
rest. A Newtonian viscous stress requires a velocity gradient, as we derive in
[Chapter 2](/learn/fluid-mechanics/mass-and-momentum/).

## Balance a horizontal slice

Choose $z$ upward and gravitational acceleration $\boldsymbol g=-g\boldsymbol e_z$.
Take a stationary slice of area $A$ and height $\mathrm dz$. Pressure below pushes
upward, pressure above pushes downward, and weight acts downward:

$$
p(z)A-p(z+\mathrm dz)A-\rho gA\,\mathrm dz=0.
$$

Dividing by $A\,\mathrm dz$ and taking the limit gives

$$
\frac{\mathrm dp}{\mathrm dz}=-\rho g.
$$

If the free surface is at $z=H$ and has pressure $p_{\rm atm}$, then

$$
p(z)=p_{\rm atm}+\rho g(H-z).
$$

For an illustrative constant density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and
$g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, the gauge pressure one metre below the surface is
$9810\,\mathrm{Pa}$. This value follows from the balance and chosen inputs;
it is not a numerical-solver result.

## Integrate the load

Consider a vertical rectangular gate of width $b$, extending from the surface
to depth $H$, with atmospheric pressure on its other side. At depth $h$, the
net force on a strip of height $\mathrm dh$ is
$\mathrm dF=\rho ghb\,\mathrm dh$. Therefore

$$
F=\int_0^H\rho ghb\,\mathrm dh=\frac{\rho gbH^2}{2}.
$$

The depth of the resultant follows by equating moments about the surface:

$$
h_{\rm cp}=\frac{\int_0^H h\,\mathrm dF}{F}=\frac{2H}{3}.
$$

For $b=H=1\,\mathrm m$, the load is $4905\,\mathrm N$, acting two thirds of
a metre below the surface. The resultant lies below the midpoint because the
lower strips carry larger loads.

## Pressure reference and physical pressure

Gauge pressure subtracts a reference. When both sides of the gate see the same
atmosphere, that contribution cancels in the net force. It would not cancel if
the back side were evacuated. A constant shift in a mathematical pressure
unknown is harmless only when the problem's boundary data permit the same shift.
Absolute pressure also matters for physical questions such as cavitation, outside
this incompressible teaching model.

The cylinder workflow later uses a prescribed outlet traction. Do not add an
arbitrary pressure shift while holding that traction fixed: you would change the
boundary-value problem.

## Try it and diagnose a failure

1. Double the liquid depth. Predict the bottom gauge pressure and gate force.
2. Integrate the pressure over a gate extending from depths $h_0$ to $h_1$.
3. Replace the upward coordinate with downward depth. Derive the pressure
   derivative again before writing a sign.
4. Someone multiplies bottom pressure by the whole gate area. Why is the result
   wrong, and by what factor for a gate starting at the surface?

**Check your reasoning.** In exercise 1, pressure doubles and force quadruples.
In exercise 4, the result is twice the correct force because the mean gauge
pressure is half the bottom value. These are checks of the model, independent
of mesh or software.
